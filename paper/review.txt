Summary
The paper proposes a novel automated curriculum learning framework for multi-hop question answering by refining the proposer's reward signal in a self-play setup. Existing self-play methods often reward the question proposer based purely on the solver's pass rate, which can lead to the generation of superficially difficult, unanswerable, or logically incomplete questions. To address this bottleneck, the authors introduce a backward verifier that traces the reasoning steps in reverse to ensure the generated questions are logically consistent and grounded in retrieved evidence. This delayed verification score is incorporated into the proposer's Hop-Grouped Relative Policy Optimization (HRPO) objective. The authors demonstrate that their approach yields consistent Exact Match (EM) improvements across several single-hop and multi-hop QA benchmarks compared to baselines.

Strengths
High significance and solid motivation: The paper addresses a critical bottleneck in data-free self-evolution and reinforcement learning from AI feedback (RLAIF)—reward hacking by the proposer. By ensuring questions are 'verifiably difficult,' the method improves the stability and quality of synthetic training curricula.

Novel integration of backward verification: While backward reasoning is a known concept, creatively repurposing it as a delayed reward signal for the proposer in an iterative self-play reinforcement learning loop is a novel and highly effective architectural contribution.

Consistent empirical gains: The proposed method demonstrates consistent performance improvements across a wide range of single-hop and multi-hop QA benchmarks, effectively preventing the early curriculum saturation observed in standard self-play loops.

Practical evaluation metrics: The introduction of the Hop Necessity Rate (HNR) and LLM-based validity scoring provides valuable, quantifiable mechanisms to measure whether generated questions genuinely require multi-step reasoning, directly addressing the well-known 'shortcut reasoning' problem.

Weaknesses
The abstract contains an unfilled placeholder ('improving exact match by [X]%') on line 18. Please consider updating this with the correct quantitative result prior to publication.

There is a chronological ambiguity in the training loop description (lines 124-126). The text states the proposer is updated 'first' using the HRPO reward, and the generated questions are 'then' used to train the solver. Since the difficulty reward depends on the solver's pass rate over the generated questions, it is mathematically unclear how the proposer can be updated before the solver interacts with those questions. Clarifying this sequence would improve reproducibility.

The inference prompt (Appendix C.1) strictly instructs the model to 'Verify by reasoning BACKWARD', whereas the GRPO training prompt (Appendix C.2) provides a generic forward verification instruction without any backward-specific directives.
